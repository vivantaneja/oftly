# Memory engines and a research-led product direction

Reviewed 10 November 2026. The useful objective is **delayed recall and transfer per minute of study**. A model’s prediction score, an app’s age, and a fast review session do not by themselves establish that objective. No source reviewed identifies a universally optimal engine for every subject and learner.

## What is genuinely new

| Candidate | What changes | Assessment for this local app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FSRS-7 | Fractional-day intervals, a richer forgetting curve, recency-weighted fitting and scheduling penalties | Worth evaluating when a maintained integration is ready; the benchmark variant must not be confused with the installed native engine |
| SBD, 2026 preprint | Seven parameters; stability, brittleness and difficulty; interpretable dynamics and planning | Best lightweight research candidate; prospective validation is still needed |
| RWKV/GRU/LSTM memory predictors | Learn richer patterns from review sequences; some use cross-card context | Prediction promise; deployment cost and scheduling behavior need separate evaluation |
| KAR³L + delta teaching policy, EMNLP 2024 | Uses related card content/history and estimates the gain from reviewing a candidate | The most relevant conceptual direction for a new learning app; small, short online study limits claims |
| Anki Star | Mixed-card authoring, sources, assistant handoff and extra practice | Useful product infrastructure; no replacement memory model |

The [current public benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark) includes 10,000 users and about 727 million logged reviews before filtering. With same-day reviews included, reported log loss is 0.3188 for FSRS-7 with recency and scheduling penalties and 0.3218 for SBD. RWKV-Instant reports 0.2660, but uses different features and a different split protocol; it also lacks an ordinary monotonic forgetting curve. Treat these as prediction results, not an apples-to-apples trial of study efficiency. Per-user fitted models must also be distinguished from defaults. These methodological differences prevent selecting a universal winner from the first row of a leaderboard.

[SBD’s paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10548v1) gives a compact forgetting model and discusses planning under a review budget. Its state includes how incomplete the last review’s repair was, in addition to stability and difficulty. In its own test set, fitted SBD has BCE 0.3279 versus 0.3237 for FSRS-7, using seven rather than 34 parameters. That is an appealing complexity tradeoff, not evidence of higher delayed recall. The authors explicitly identify live comparisons as future work and explain why data generated by one scheduling policy cannot prove reliable extrapolation under another. Recommendation: make SBD the first independent model to evaluate, using preserved native review events; do not quietly replace a learner’s due dates with an unvalidated implementation.

[KAR³L](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12291v3) retrieves related previously studied cards and applies a policy based on predicted benefit from reviewing now. The online evaluation used 27 learners, 32 six-day sessions, and small fixed trivia sets. Post-test accuracy was 0.86 for KAR³L versus 0.88 for FSRS; average response time across cards was 6.27 versus 6.82 seconds, with the latter difference reported significant. This supports further investigation of content-aware teaching. It does not establish higher accuracy, semester-long retention, or a production-ready semantic engine for arbitrary coursework. A keyword shuffle or a prompt generator would not reproduce its trained model.

## Cross-check with learning studies

A [2025 meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10076-6) compares retrieval with active elaborative learning, not merely rereading. Across 44 studies and 142 comparisons, the overall advantage was small (g=0.14), and feedback mattered substantially. This argues for a product that combines retrieval with useful explanations and correction. It does not justify replacing all learning activities with rapid fact flips.

A [2026 classroom meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-026-10225-5) finds benefits for retrieval, spacing and interleaving with context-dependent moderators. Its effects use different comparisons, so they are not a ranking of three algorithms. In this app, comparison questions should target meaningfully confusable concepts; randomly shuffling unrelated cards is not equivalent to that intervention.

[Successive relearning research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27027887/) supports returning to material over separated sessions. Extra same-session drilling is not interchangeable with later retrieval. Consequently, two immediate correct answers should not produce a durable “mastered” claim. Native due dates and sibling separation remain useful foundations.

## What we built from this evidence

The production temporal model remains native FSRS, with real review logging and a 90% initial requested retention. The distinct learning layer now improves **what is practised and corrected**:

- A source-grounded prompt builder can request explanation, comparison and application questions, with answer rubrics and corrective explanations. The user checks the drafts before import; a source reference is not proof the draft is correct.
- The app preserves explanatory feedback and references as first-class note fields, and presents them after retrieval rather than mixing IDs and boilerplate into the question.
- Typed recall works for imported typed-answer cards. The learner compares meaning and rates recall; exact spelling is not used as a universal correctness criterion.
- Optional authoring suggestions identify multiple questions, long targets, vague cues, difficult cloze structure and missing references. Every kind and the entire feature can be disabled. These are transparent writing heuristics, not a validated language model or a claim that 60 words is a scientific limit.

These features are implemented. SBD, FSRS-7 benchmark variants and KAR³L are research candidates, not silently installed schedulers. We do not claim these capabilities are absent from every Anki add-on; the distinction is a coherent, simple learning workflow in this app.

## How to choose the next engine with evidence

First, replay preserved review events with chronological holdouts. Fit only on preceding events and compare calibration, log loss and inference time; separate cold-start and established users. Version model parameters and report excluded or incomplete histories. Second, test candidate schedules prospectively with explicit assignment, keeping content and workload comparable. Measure recall after a week and a month, time spent, lapses and transfer questions that were not simply repeated verbatim. Keep recognition, typed recall and assisted practice separately labelled. A promising offline score earns an experiment; superior delayed recall per minute earns a new default.

Content-aware scheduling also needs stable concept relationships and clean exposure records. Source fields, topic tags, preserved IDs and explicit card types are useful groundwork. They are not a substitute for training and evaluating the actual model. This is the next substantive research step beyond a reskinned flashcard scheduler.
